# 03 — Terminal Value Functions: when is concave PWL `V(SOC)` exact, and what is the error when it is not

Scope: conditions under which the storage value-to-go is provably concave in energy content; where it provably fails
for a behind-the-meter battery with peak charges and qualification cliffs; error propagation bounds; MILP
exactness of the concave-PWL representation; multi-dimensional state.

---

## Provable under stated assumptions

### A. Concavity of the value function

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The optimal cost of an LP is a **convex piecewise-linear** function of the right-hand-side vector `b`, on the (polyhedral) domain where the primal is feasible and the dual bounded. Its subdifferential at `b` is the set of dual optimal solutions. For a **maximisation** LP the optimal value is **concave PWL** in `b`. | Finite LP; `b` enters only the RHS; objective and constraint matrix fixed. No integer variables. | Bertsimas & Tsitsiklis, *Introduction to Linear Optimization*, Athena Scientific 1997, Ch. 5 (§5.2 "Global dependence on the RHS vector", Thm 5.1); Bertsekas, *Convex Optimization Theory*, §5.4. |
| A2 | **Partial minimisation preserves convexity.** If `f(x,y)` is jointly convex and `C` convex, then `g(x) = inf_{y∈C} f(x,y)` is convex. Dually, partial maximisation of a jointly concave function over a convex set is concave. This is the master theorem behind every concavity result below; A1 is its LP special case. | Joint convexity/concavity; `C` convex and independent of `x` (or a convex graph in `(x,y)`); `g > −∞`. | Boyd & Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, CUP 2004, §3.2.5. https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/ |
| A3 | **Two-stage recourse:** for a stochastic LP with **fixed recourse**, `Q(x,ξ) = min{q'y : Wy = h(ξ) − T(ξ)x, y ≥ 0}` is convex polyhedral in `x`; `Q(x) = E_ξ[Q(x,ξ)]` is convex; and when `ξ` has **finite discrete support**, `Q(x)` is **convex piecewise linear** (a finite nonnegative combination of convex polyhedral functions). Expectation preserves convexity. | Fixed recourse matrix `W` (independent of `ξ`); relatively complete recourse or an explicit induced-feasibility set; finite `Ξ` for the PWL part; **no integer second-stage variables**. | Birge & Louveaux, *Introduction to Stochastic Programming*, 2nd ed., Springer 2011, Ch. 3 (Thm 3.1 / Prop. 3.x: `Q(·,ξ)` convex polyhedral in `x`; Thm 3.7: `Q` convex, PWL for finite `Ξ`). https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4614-0237-4 |
| A4 | **Multistage recurrence.** If the stage reward is concave in `(state, action)`, the feasible set is convex, the transition is affine, and the disturbance is exogenous (stagewise independent or exogenous Markov), then `V_t(s) = max_{a∈A(s)} { r(s,a) + E V_{t+1}(f(s,a,w)) }` is concave in `s` by induction: `V_{t+1}` concave ∘ affine `f` is concave; `E` of concave is concave; sum of concave is concave; partial max over a convex set (A2) is concave. This is exactly the assumption set SDDP requires for cut validity. | Concave stage reward; convex constraint set with convex graph in `(s,a)`; **affine** state transition; exogenous noise; **no integer variables and no fixed costs coupling the state**; `V_T` concave terminal condition. | Pereira & Pinto, "Multi-stage stochastic optimization applied to energy planning", *Math. Prog.* 52:359–375 (1991), https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01582895 ; Füllner & Rebennack, *SDDP and its variants — a review*, https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/SDDP-Review.pdf |
| A5 | **Storage-specific, lossy case.** With SOC dynamics `x_{t+1} = λx_t + Δ(η_c u_t^+ + (1/η_d) u_t^−)`, `η_c, η_d ∈ (0,1]`, the map `φ: u ↦ x` is **concave and invertible**, `φ^{-1}` is **convex** (Thm 1), and the set of feasible **energy** profiles `X = {x : φ^{-1}(x) ∈ [−ū, u], x ∈ [x̲, x̄]}` is **always a convex polytope** (Thm 2) with an explicit half-space representation. Hence: eliminating the charge/discharge split and working in SOC space removes the nonconvexity **exactly** — not as an inner or outer approximation. | `0 < η_c ≤ 1/η_d`; box bounds on SOC and power; single storage unit; no transmission constraints. | Al Taha & Bitar, "When are Lossy Energy Storage Optimization Models Convex?", arXiv:2403.14010v2, **Thms 1–2**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14010 |
| A6 | **Concavity of the storage objective — the sharp condition.** If the cost `c: R^T → R` is **convex** and **coordinate-wise nondecreasing on `[0,∞)`** (i.e. nondecreasing in the **charging** power; monotonicity in discharging is *not* required), then `c ∘ φ^{-1}` is **convex** in the SOC profile. With A5 (convex polytope) and A2 (partial minimisation), the value-to-go is **convex in cost / concave in profit as a function of SOC**. This unifies and generalises prior conditions that required smoothness, piecewise affinity, or monotonicity in *both* power directions. | As A5, plus `c` convex and coordinate-wise nondecreasing on the nonnegative orthant. | Al Taha & Bitar, arXiv:2403.14010v2, **Thm 3**. |
| A7 | **Exchange-argument corollary (one line, derivable).** One slot: import `x` at effective price `p^imp`, gaining `η_c x` of SOC; export `y` from SOC, delivering `η_d y` at `p^exp`. An SOC-neutral simultaneous pair (`y = η_c x`) changes cash by `x(η_c η_d p^exp − p^imp)`. It is strictly unprofitable **iff `η_c η_d p^exp_t < p^imp_t`**. With `η_c η_d < 1` and `p^exp ≤ p^imp` (the BTM norm: import carries grid fees and levies that export does not) this holds strictly whenever `p^imp_t > 0`. | Price-taker; **no objective term rewarding gross throughput** — no reserve payment on gross power, no volumetric credit, no accumulator counting gross kWh. | Derived; consistent with Al Taha & Bitar Thm 3 (`c` nondecreasing in charging ⇔ `p^imp ≥ 0`) and Prat et al. Prop. 1 (B1). |

### B. Exactness of dropping the charge/discharge complementarity

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Prop. 1.** Let `T^-` = periods with strictly negative price, `T^0` = zero price. (i) If `T^- ∪ T^0 = ∅` **and** `η < 1`, then **every** optimal LP-relaxation solution satisfies complementarity — the relaxation is exact and the binary is provably redundant. (ii) If `η = 1`, *some* optimal LP solution satisfies complementarity (not all). (iii) If `T^- = ∅`, *some* optimal LP solution satisfies complementarity. | Price-taker storage, finite horizon, SOC and power boxes, round-trip efficiency `η = η_c η_d`, objective linear in prices. | Prat, Lusby & Pinson, "Sufficient A Priori Conditions for the Linear Relaxation of the Energy Storage Scheduling Problem", arXiv:2508.12999, **Prop. 1**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12999 |
| B2 | **Cor. 1.** Under `η < 1` and `T^- ≠ ∅`: if an optimal solution exhibits simultaneous charge/discharge in period `t`, then **`t ∈ T^- ∪ T^0`**; and if `t ∈ T^0` an equivalent optimum without it exists. ⇒ **Binaries are needed only in strictly-negative-price slots.** | As B1. | Prat et al., arXiv:2508.12999, **Cor. 1**. |
| B3 | **Cor. 2 (inexactness).** Under `η < 1`, `T^- ≠ ∅`, the relaxation is **inexact** if (1) `ρ S̲ + Δt·η_c·P̄^C > S̄` and (2) `ρ S̄ − Δt·(1/η_d)·P̄^D < S̲` — with no leakage (`ρ=1`) this reduces to `Δt^C < Δt` **and** `Δt^D < Δt`: the charge duration **and** the discharge duration are both shorter than one time slot. A very fast battery on a coarse slot is exactly the inexact regime. | As B1; the simplified form assumes `ρ = 1`. | Prat et al., arXiv:2508.12999, **Cor. 2**. |
| B4 | **Thms 2–3 (a priori exactness despite negative prices).** With one (Thm 2) or several (Thm 3) maximal runs of strictly negative prices, the relaxation is exact if, for each run, starting from the lowest reachable SOC and net-charging at maximum rate through the run, the **upper SOC bound is not exceeded**. Intuition: if the battery can absorb the whole negative-price run by genuine net charging, it never needs the fake simultaneous mode. Conditions are checkable **before** solving. | Assumptions 1–2 of the paper; explicit leakage `ρ`; single-run (Thm 2) / multi-run (Thm 3). | Prat et al., arXiv:2508.12999, **Thms 2 and 3**. |
| B5 | The **single-period** convex hull of the storage operation set is known in closed form (vertex and hyperplane representations). LP relaxations of the standard two-binary formulation are **not** the convex hull for **multi-period** problems, and no polynomial-size tight multi-period formulation is known (facet families grow exponentially). | Single time period for the hull result; basic operation plus reserves/investment extensions. | Pozo, "Convex Hull Formulations for Linear Modeling of Energy Storage Systems", *IEEE Trans. Power Syst.* 38(6):5934–5936, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1109/TPWRS.2023.3268135 ; Elgersma, Morales-España, Aardal, Helistö, Kiviluoma & de Weerdt, "Tight MIP Formulations for Optimal Operation and Investment of Storage Including Reserves", arXiv:2411.17484. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17484 |
| B6 | **Counterexample (Example 1).** `E̲=5, Ē=50 MWh, P^C=P^D=10 MW, η_c=η_d=0.5`, battery at `e=50`. Under negative prices the LP relaxation returns `(p^C, p^D, δ) = (8, 2, 0.8)` — exactly SOC-neutral simultaneous charge/discharge, physically unexecutable. Concavity of `V(SOC)` is irrelevant here: the **feasible set**, not the value-function shape, is what fails. | — | Elgersma et al., arXiv:2411.17484, §II Example 1. |
| B7 | **Reserves break exactness independently of price sign.** Even in the tightened (convex-hull-per-period) formulation, the LP relaxation still provides **down reserves by charging while discharging**. Reserve co-optimisation reintroduces precisely the gross-throughput reward that A7 forbids. | Reserve products defined as headroom on top of the scheduled charge/discharge. | Elgersma et al., arXiv:2411.17484, §V.A and Table I discussion. |

### C. Where concavity provably fails, and the state-augmentation fix

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Peak/demand charges break the principle of optimality.** An objective containing a `sup`/`max` over the trajectory (`peakPrice · max_t import_t`) is **not additively separable**; the Bellman recursion on the un-augmented state does **not** yield an optimal policy. | Finite-horizon DP; objective contains a supremum over stages. | Jones & Peet, "Solving Dynamic Programming with Supremum Terms in the Objective and Application to Optimal Battery Scheduling for Electricity Consumers Subject to Demand Charges", arXiv:1708.03714 / *IEEE CDC 2017*, https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2017.8263838 ; extended in arXiv:1812.00792. |
| C2 | **State augmentation restores it.** The supremum is a special case of **forward-separable** objectives. For **any** forward-separable objective there exists an **augmented-state DP** which satisfies the principle of optimality and whose solutions yield solutions of the original problem. The tractable subclass is **Naturally Forward Separable (NFS)**; the running peak `P̄_t = max(P̄_{t−1}, import_t)` is NFS with a **1-dimensional** augmentation. | Forward separability of the objective; augmentation dimension = number of `sup` terms. | Jones & Peet, arXiv:1708.03714 (main theorem); arXiv:1812.00792. |
| C3 | **The augmented value function is jointly concave in `(SOC, P̄)` in the LP case.** With the peak modelled as `zPeak ≥ P̄`, `zPeak ≥ import_t`, objective `− peakPrice·zPeak·proration`, both `SOC_0` and `P̄` enter **only as right-hand sides of linear constraints**, so A1 applies verbatim: the LP value is concave PWL **jointly** in `(SOC, P̄)`. Concavity in `SOC` for fixed `P̄` is the strictly weaker marginal statement. | Pure LP over the coupling (no binaries linking `SOC` or `P̄`); `P̄`, `SOC_0` appear only in RHS. | Direct corollary of A1 (Bertsimas & Tsitsiklis Thm 5.1) applied to the augmented state. |
| C4 | **Lipschitz modulus in the peak coordinate.** `V*` is nondecreasing in `P̄` with `0 ≤ ∂V*/∂P̄ ≤ peakPrice · proration` (an already-incurred higher peak can only relax the remaining problem, and the relaxation is worth at most the peak price per kW). Hence `|V*(e, P̄_1) − V*(e, P̄_2)| ≤ peakPrice · proration · |P̄_1 − P̄_2|`. | Peak charge enters linearly with coefficient `peakPrice·proration`; no other dependence on `P̄`. | Derived; the subgradient bound is the dual-variable bound of A1. |
| C5 | **Discrete qualification cliffs destroy concavity, and mp-MILP theory states exactly how.** The value function of a **multiparametric MILP** is, *for each fixed integer vector*, convex piecewise affine on a polyhedral partition; the overall value function is the pointwise `min` (`max`, for maximisation) over integer vectors of these — piecewise affine, but in general **non-convex/non-concave**, possibly **discontinuous**, over a partition that need not be convex. This **is** the formal justification for "concave conditional on a discrete state": condition on the integer vector (the `qualState` label), keep exact concavity inside each cell, accept that the envelope across cells is not concave. | Value function of an mp-MILP; parameters in the RHS; bounded feasible region. | Dua & Pistikopoulos, "An algorithm for the solution of multiparametric mixed integer linear programming problems", *Annals of OR* 99:123–139, 2000, https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1019212328920 ; Acevedo & Pistikopoulos, *Ind. Eng. Chem. Res.* 36:717–728, 1997; Borrelli, Bemporad & Morari, *Predictive Control for Linear and Hybrid Systems*, CUP 2017, Ch. 6. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781139061759 |
| C6 | **Inventory-theory analogue: fixed costs destroy concavity, and only a weaker property survives.** With a fixed setup cost `K` the profit-to-go is **not** concave; Scarf's `K`-convexity is the exact replacement property that survives the DP induction and yields optimality of `(s,S)` policies. Without setup cost the value function is convex (cost sense) and base-stock is optimal. `K`-convexity **fails** once the setup cost is quantity-dependent or state-dependent, and `(s,S)` is then not optimal. | Periodic review, convex holding/shortage cost, fixed + linear ordering cost, i.i.d. demand, finite or discounted infinite horizon. | Scarf, "The Optimality of (S,s) Policies in the Dynamic Inventory Problem", in *Mathematical Methods in the Social Sciences*, Stanford UP 1960; Zabel, *Mgmt Sci.* 9(1):123–125, 1962 (corrections); Benjaafar, Chen & Yu, "Optimal policies for inventory systems with concave ordering costs", *Naval Res. Logistics*, 2018, https://doi.org/10.1002/nav.21808 |

### D. Error propagation: what an inexact `V̂` costs

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Discounted infinite horizon — the standard bound.** Let `π` be greedy w.r.t. `v` (`T_π v = T v`). Then `v^π ≥ v* − (2γ/(1−γ))·‖v* − v‖_∞ · 1`. The constant is **`2γ/(1−γ)`**, not `2/(1−γ)`. The bound is **tight** — attained by an explicit two-state MDP. | Stationary discounted MDP, `γ ∈ [0,1)`; sup-norm error; arbitrary greedy tie-breaking; bounded rewards. | Singh & Yee, "An Upper Bound on the Loss from Approximate Optimal-Value Functions", *Machine Learning* 16(3):227–233, 1994, https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022693225949 ; statement + tightness proof: https://rltheory.github.io/lecture-notes/planning-in-mdps/lec3/ |
| D2 | **Q-form.** If `π` is greedy w.r.t. `Q` then `V^π(s) ≥ V*(s) − (2/(1−γ))·‖Q − Q*‖_∞`. Note the **missing `γ`** relative to D1 — the two forms are not interchangeable, and quoting one constant for the other is a factor-`1/γ` error. | As D1. | Singh & Yee 1994; Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis, *Neuro-Dynamic Programming*, Athena Scientific 1996, §6.1 / Prop. 6.1. http://www.athenasc.com/ndpbook.html |
| D3 | **Approximate VI/PI accumulate a second factor.** For approximate value or policy iteration with per-iteration sup-norm error `ε`, the asymptotic loss is `2γε/(1−γ)²`. The extra `1/(1−γ)` is the cost of *iterating*, not of a single greedy step. Widely regarded as conservative in practice. | Discounted MDP; error `ε` in sup norm at **every** iteration. | Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis, *NDP* 1996, Props 6.2–6.3; de Farias & Van Roy, "Performance Loss Bounds for Approximate Value Iteration with State Aggregation", https://web.stanford.edu/~bvr/pubs/aggregation.pdf |
| D4 | **Finite horizon / one-step lookahead with an approximate terminal cost — the bound that actually applies here.** Solve the `H`-step problem exactly with terminal value `V̂`, `‖V̂ − V*_H‖_∞ ≤ ε`. Execute the first decision, thereafter act optimally. Achieved value `≥ J*(x) − 2ε`. Proof: the perturbed objective differs uniformly from the true one by at most `ε`, so (a) the perturbed optimum over-states the true value of its own plan by ≤ `ε`, and (b) it under-states the true optimum's value by ≤ `ε`. **No `1/(1−γ)` blow-up: the loss is `2ε`, once, per terminal boundary.** | Undiscounted or discounted **finite** horizon; **all** in-horizon rewards and dynamics exact; error confined to the terminal value; truncated problem solved to optimality. | Elementary sandwich argument; discounted stationary analogue is D1. One-step lookahead = one Newton step on Bellman's equation, with the stability region shrinking in the terminal-cost error: Bertsekas, *Lessons from AlphaZero for Optimal, Model Predictive, and Adaptive Control*, Athena Scientific 2022, Ch. 1. http://www.athenasc.com/Lessons.html |
| D5 | **Receding-horizon closed loop.** Re-solving every slot with the same `V̂` does **not** compound linearly in the number of re-solves when the terminal boundary is a genuine reset point of the augmented state (month end resets `peakState` and the accumulators). The `2ε` of D4 is incurred **per accounting period**, plus a coupling term through the one state that does *not* reset: `SOC`. MPC suboptimality bounds tie the gap jointly to (i) terminal-cost approximation error, (ii) terminal-set size, (iii) horizon length. | Terminal cost approximates the true period-/infinite-horizon value; recursive feasibility and stabilisability conditions of MPC theory. | Moreno-Mora, Beckenbach & Streif, "Predictive Control with Learning-Based Terminal Costs Using Approximate Value Iteration", arXiv:2212.00361, *IFAC WC 2023*, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2023.10.1240 |
| D6 | **All of D1–D5 require a *sup-norm* error over the states actually visited, on the *same* state space.** They are vacuous if `V̂` is compared to `V*` only on a sampled subset, or if `V*` lives on a **richer state space** than `V̂` is indexed by. A model-mismatch / state-projection term is **not** covered by any of them and must be bounded separately. | — | Standard caveat, explicit in de Farias & Van Roy (weighted-norm counterexamples). |

### E. Concave-hull projection: what fitting error you buy

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **The concave hull is an over-estimate — provable by definition.** `conc(V) := inf{ g : g concave, g ≥ V }` is the smallest concave majorant, so `conc(V)(e) ≥ V(e)` for all `e`, with equality only where `V` touches its hull. Projecting a non-concave fitted `V` onto the concave hull therefore yields a **pointwise optimistic** terminal value. | `V` bounded above on a convex domain. | Rockafellar, *Convex Analysis*, Princeton UP 1970, §12 (convex/concave envelopes); definitional. |
| E2 | **The MILP LP relaxation performs exactly the same projection.** For a **minimisation** with separable nonconvex PWL costs, the LP relaxation of **all three** textbook MIP formulations (incremental/delta, multiple-choice/disaggregated convex combination, and convex-combination/SOS2) approximates the cost by its **lower convex envelope**; the three relaxations are equivalent and the result is linked to classical Lagrangian duality. Negating: for a **maximisation** with nonconcave PWL rewards, the LP relaxation returns the **upper concave envelope**. Consequence: **hand-fitting the concave hull and relying on the LP relaxation of an honest non-concave PWL give the same optimistic function.** | Separable PWL costs; the three named formulations; bounded variables. | Croxton, Gendron & Magnanti, "A Comparison of Mixed-Integer Programming Models for Nonconvex Piecewise Linear Cost Minimization Problems", *Management Science* 49(9):1268–1273, 2003. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.49.9.1268.16570 |
| E3 | **Optimism ⇒ bias, but through the *slope*, not the *level*.** Adding a constant to `V` does not change the greedy policy; the policy depends only on `λ_SOC = ∂V/∂e`. The concave hull replaces the true slope by the **chord slope** across each non-concave interval: **below** the chord it over-states the marginal value of stored energy (⇒ over-storage / over-buying), **above** it under-states it (⇒ premature discharge). Net effect is a **pull toward the non-concavity** (the cliff), **not** a uniform over-storage. Claims of "optimistic terminal value ⇒ systematic over-storage" are only half right. | `V` differentiable a.e.; greedy/lookahead policy uses `λ_SOC` as the terminal marginal. | Derived from E1–E2; level-invariance of greedy policies is immediate from the Bellman operator. |
| E4 | **The same optimism is structural in cutting-plane ADP.** SDDP's Benders cuts form an **outer** approximation of the cost-to-go — a **lower bound** in minimisation, i.e. an **upper bound (over-estimate) in maximisation**. The deterministic master-problem bound is therefore *optimistic*; the honest bound must be estimated statistically out-of-sample. Any planner reading a cut-based `V̂` is reading an optimistic terminal value **by construction**, before any hull projection. | Convex stage problems; valid cuts; stagewise independence for the classical algorithm. | Pereira & Pinto 1991, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01582895 ; Füllner & Rebennack review, https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/SDDP-Review.pdf ; Philpott & Guan, *Oper. Res. Lett.* 36(4):450–455, 2008, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orl.2008.01.013 |

### F. Concave PWL in a MILP: no binaries needed — direction confirmed

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **A convex PWL function is LP-representable as a `max` of its affine pieces (epigraph form); a concave PWL function is LP-representable as a `min` of its affine pieces (hypograph form).** `f(x) = max_i (a_i'x + b_i)` is modelled by `z ≥ a_i'x + b_i ∀i` while **minimising** `z`; `f(x) = min_i (a_i'x + b_i)` by `z ≤ a_i'x + b_i ∀i` while **maximising** `z`. Zero binaries, zero SOS constraints. | The direction must match: **convex-with-minimisation** or **concave-with-maximisation**. The opposite pairing (concave-minimisation / convex-maximisation) is the NP-hard one. | Boyd & Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, §3.2.3 (pointwise max/min), §4.3.1 (LP representability). https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/ |
| F2 | **λ- / convex-combination (SOS2) formulation: LP relaxation is exact in the easy direction.** Writing `e = Σ λ_i e_i`, `V = Σ λ_i v_i`, `Σ λ_i = 1`, `λ ≥ 0` **without** SOS2/binaries yields exactly the **concave envelope** of the breakpoint data (by E2). If `{(e_i, v_i)}` is already concave (strictly decreasing slopes) the envelope **equals** the function ⇒ **the LP relaxation is exact and the binaries are provably redundant.** | Maximisation objective; breakpoints in increasing `e`; strictly decreasing slopes `(v_{i+1}−v_i)/(e_{i+1}−e_i)`. | Croxton, Gendron & Magnanti 2003 (envelope result); Vielma, Ahmed & Nemhauser, "Mixed-Integer Models for Nonseparable Piecewise-Linear Optimization: Unifying Framework and Extensions", *Operations Research* 58(2):303–315, 2010, https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1090.0721 (PDF: https://juan-pablo-vielma.github.io/publications/Mixed-Integer-Models-for-Nonseparable.pdf); Vielma, "Mixed Integer Linear Programming Formulation Techniques", *SIAM Review* 57(1):3–57, 2015, https://doi.org/10.1137/130915303 ; Huchette & Vielma, *Oper. Res.* 71(5):1835–1856, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.2019.1973 |
| F3 | **The spec's direction is the easy one.** The objective maximises EUR; `V(SOC)` is concave; concave-maximisation is exactly the LP-representable pairing of F1. The spec's claim is **correct**, and it is the standard result, not a special trick. | The strictly-decreasing-slope contract must actually be enforced — one non-decreasing slope pair silently converts the model into the envelope of E2 rather than raising an error. | F1–F2. |

## Applicability to the target system

**Blunt verdict: the concave-PWL `V(SOC)` is exact for the energy-arbitrage core of this system, and provably inexact for exactly the two features the spec added discrete state dimensions to handle. One of those two errors is bounded and priceable; the other is not.**

| Spec element | Verdict | Justification | Error character |
|---|---|---|---|
| `V(SOC)` concave PWL, LP relaxation without binaries, maximisation objective | **Exact and standard.** | F1–F3. Concave-maximisation is the LP-representable pairing. `min`-of-affine-pieces or the plain λ-formulation both work; the LP relaxation is the function itself, not an envelope — **provided** the strictly-decreasing-slope contract holds. | Zero, conditional on the contract. |
| `λ_SOC` = subgradient of `V` at optimal terminal SOC, as an **output** | **Exactly right — and it is the dual variable of the terminal SOC balance constraint.** Publishing `λ_SOC` as an *input* would be the error; the spec avoids it. | A1: the subdifferential of an LP value w.r.t. an RHS is the dual optimal set. | Zero. Caveat: the subgradient is **set-valued at a breakpoint** — the contract must state which element (left slope, right slope, or midpoint) is published. |
| Concavity of `V` in SOC for the arbitrage core (import/export, `η_c`, `η_d`, SOC/power boxes) | **Provably concave.** | A5 + A6 (Al Taha & Bitar Thms 1–3): the feasible SOC-profile set is **always** a convex polytope; the objective is convex in the SOC profile whenever the cost is convex and nondecreasing in **charging** power, i.e. whenever the effective **import** price is `≥ 0`. A2/A4 then give concavity of the value-to-go. | Zero. |
| Import/export complementarity binaries per slot | **Redundant whenever `p^imp_t > 0` for all `t` (B1(i)); necessary only in slots where `η_c η_d p^exp_t ≥ p^imp_t` (A7, B2).** For BTM, `p^imp` carries grid fees and levies that `p^exp` does not, so the condition holds far more robustly than for a wholesale-facing battery. | Prat et al. Prop. 1, Cor. 1. | Zero in positive-price slots. **Note: concavity of `V(SOC)` is unaffected either way** — the binary is a feasible-set issue, not a value-function-shape issue. Do not conflate them. |
| Monthly peak charge `peakPrice·zPeak·proration` | **Breaks the principle of optimality on the un-augmented state (C1); the spec's `peakState` augmentation is the correct and formally justified fix (C2).** However `V` is **jointly concave in `(SOC, P̄)` as a continuous pair (C3)** — discretising `P̄` into a label is a **strict and unnecessary loss**; concavity never required it. | C1–C3. | **Bounded.** `|V*(e, P̄) − V*(e, centre(bin))| ≤ peakPrice · proration · (bin width)` by C4; closed-loop loss at most twice that per month by D4. A 5 kW bin at 100 €/kW·month bounds terminal-value misvaluation at 500 €/month. **Halving the bin halves the bound.** This error is engineered, priced, and controllable. |
| Grid-fee qualification cliff → separate discrete `qualState` dimension, concave `V` within each state | **The construction is formally correct — it is exactly the mp-MILP value-function structure (C5):** convex/concave PW-affine for each fixed integer vector, non-concave and possibly discontinuous across them. The spec is right that concavity survives only *conditional* on the label. Inventory theory gives the same lesson: a discrete regime switch (fixed cost) destroys concavity and only a weaker property survives (C6). | C5, C6. | **See the next row — this is where the bound dies.** |
| Seven month-to-date accumulators are **continuous** planner variables, but `V` is conditioned on them only through **discrete** `qualState`/`peakState` labels | **This is the unbounded error, and no bound in the literature covers it.** | D1–D5 require `‖V̂ − V*‖_∞` over the **true** state space. Here `V̂` is indexed by `(SOC, label(m))` while `V*` is a function of `(SOC, m)`, `m ∈ R^7`. The projection error is `sup_{m ∈ cell} |V*(e,m) − V*(e, label(m))|`, finite **only if `V*` is Lipschitz in `m` on that cell**. At a qualification threshold `V*` **jumps** by the full regime-change value `Δfee` — that discontinuity *is* the cliff. No finite Lipschitz modulus exists across the threshold. | **Unbounded in the Lipschitz sense.** Bounded by `Δfee` **only** if the label boundary is placed **exactly** at the threshold. Even then the label is **not a sufficient statistic**: two states on the same side of the threshold with different *headroom* to it have genuinely different `V*`, and the planner cannot distinguish them. The residual error is the option value of the remaining headroom — up to `Δfee`. Contrast the peak state, where C4 gives a clean linear bound. |
| Introducing a second **continuous** coordinate (a month-to-date accumulator) into `V` | **Nothing breaks in principle.** A1/A2/C3 give joint concavity in `(SOC, m)` for the LP part, and cut / λ-formulations extend to `R^n` — SDDP is built on exactly this. What breaks is **representation and contract**: a concave PWL on `R^{1+k}` is no longer a breakpoint list with a slope-monotonicity check. The contract becomes "a finite set of affine pieces, `V = min_i (α_i·e + β_i'·m + γ_i)`", and validation is no longer a one-dimensional slope test. | A1 (concave PWL in the full RHS vector), F1 (`min`-of-affine works in any dimension), Pereira & Pinto (multi-dimensional Benders cuts). | Zero approximation error; instead a contract-schema cost and a fitting sample-complexity cost (cut count grows with dimension). The `min`-of-affine form is the correct generalisation and keeps the LP relaxation exact (F1). |
| Slow loop writes `V` to a store; planner **reads at a lag** | The lag is an error term **outside** every bound above: it enters as staleness of the price/load distribution, i.e. **model** error, not value-function error. D1–D5 all assume the in-horizon model is exact. | D6. | Must be bounded separately (re-fit frequency vs. price-process drift). **No cited theorem covers it.** |

**Three consequences the spec should absorb:**

1. **The concave-hull projection is not an extra sin — it is the LP relaxation made honest (E2).** Feed a genuinely non-concave breakpoint set to a λ-formulation without binaries and the solver silently uses the concave envelope anyway (Croxton–Gendron–Magnanti). Enforcing the hull at fit time and validating strictly decreasing slopes at the contract boundary makes that projection **visible and testable** instead of silent. Strictly better than shipping raw breakpoints.
2. **The resulting bias is directional, not a uniform over-storage (E3).** The policy uses only `λ_SOC = ∂V/∂e`, so level optimism is policy-irrelevant; the hull's **chord slope** over-values SOC on the low side of a non-concavity and under-values it on the high side — a systematic **pull toward the qualification threshold**. Combined with E4 (cut-based ADP is optimistic by construction in a maximisation), the terminal value handed to the planner is optimistic **twice over**, and the strictly-decreasing-slope validator checks shape, not level or calibration. **The missing contract check is a calibration test:** realised value vs. `V̂` at the terminal SOC, per `(peakState, qualState)` cell.
3. **`peakState` should be continuous, `qualState` must be discrete.** C3 proves `V` is jointly concave in `(SOC, P̄)` — the peak state is the *wrong* thing to discretise, and discretising it buys a bounded but avoidable loss (C4). The qualification cliff is the *right* thing to discretise, because C5 says nothing better exists; but its cost is unbounded by Lipschitz argument and must be controlled by placing the label boundary exactly on the threshold **and** carrying at least one continuous headroom coordinate inside the concave representation.
