# MSP vs SDDP vs ADP for a storage problem with a terminal value function

Scope: what each method *requires of the uncertainty representation*, and what each *provably guarantees*. Every numeric/performance figure from a paper is quarantined in section 2 as a prediction.

---

## Provable under stated assumptions

### 1. SDDP: the stagewise-independence requirement — what it buys and what breaks without it

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Under stagewise independence the cost-to-go function `Q_t(x_{t-1})` **does not depend on the data process** — there is exactly ONE cost-to-go function per stage, shared by every node of that stage. | "the random data process is stagewise independent, i.e., random vector ξ_{t+1} is independent of ξ_[t] = (ξ_1,…,ξ_t) for t = 1,…,T−1"; relatively complete recourse; cost-to-go functions finite-valued. | Shapiro (2011), §3: *"here the cost-to-go functions Q_t(x_{t-1}) do not depend on the data process because of the stagewise independence assumption."* |
| 1.2 | **Cut sharing is valid** precisely because of 1.1: a supporting plane of `Q_t` computed at any node of stage t is valid at *every* node of stage t. Consequence: cost of one forward+backward sweep is proportional to the **sum** of sampled data points per stage, not their **product**. | As 1.1. | Shapiro (2011), §1: *"the computational complexity of one run of the involved backward and forward step procedures is proportional to the sum of sampled data points at every stage and not to the total number of scenarios given by their product… this nice property holds because of the stagewise independence assumption."* |
| 1.3 | **What breaks under stagewise dependence:** the true value function becomes `Q_t(x_{t-1}, ξ_[t])`. A cut derived on one history is then not valid on another. Validity of the outer approximation — hence validity of the deterministic lower bound and of the convergence proofs — is lost, not merely weakened. | Negation of 1.1. | Direct corollary of 1.1/1.2 (Shapiro 2011 §3); Dowson (2020) A1–A7 discussion confirms stagewise (= *nodewise*) independence of `ω` is an induced, "although unstated", requirement of the policy-graph/SDDP construction. |
| 1.4 | **AR(1) state augmentation restores validity for RHS-only uncertainty.** If `b_t = Φ b_{t-1} + ε_t` with `{ε_t}` independent and all other data stagewise independent, rewrite feasibility as `B_t x_{t-1} − Φ b_{t-1} + A_t x_t = ε_t`; replace state `x_t` by `(x_t, b_t)` and the process by `(c_t, A_t, B_t, ε_t)`. Problem becomes stagewise independent. Nonnegativity is *not* enforced on the appended `b_t`. | Uncertainty confined to the RHS `b_t`; AR is **linear with additive noise**; `Φ` deterministic. | Shapiro (2011), eq. (1.2). Same device for order-p AR: Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019) §3.2. |
| 1.5 | **State augmentation provably fails for objective-coefficient and LHS-matrix dependence.** Substituting the AR recursion introduces products of a decision variable with a state variable — bilinear terms that standard (LP-based) SDDP cannot handle, because the stage problem is no longer convex. | Uncertainty in `c_t` or in `A_t`/`B_t`. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), §3.2: *"this approach in general does not extend to the situation where uncertainty exists in the objective coefficients or left hand side matrix of constraints, because bilinear terms will be introduced but cannot be handled by the standard SDDP method."* |
| 1.6 | **Exception (and it is the key one for a MILP):** if *both* factors of the induced bilinear term are binary (after binary expansion of the state), the bilinear term is exactly linearisable. This makes objective-side stagewise dependence tractable **only** in the 0-1 state space. | Binary state variables. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), §3.2: *"these bilinear terms are products of two binary variables after reformulation using binary expansion or approximation, which can be easily reformulated as linear constraints. This is another significant advantage of considering the 0-1 state space."* |
| 1.7 | **Saddle-function SDDP for objective (price) dependence.** If the price state evolves exogenously and linearly, `y_t = B_t^ω y_{t-1} + b^ω`, and enters the objective bilinearly (`y_t^⊤ Q_t x_t`), then `V_{t+1}(x_t, y_t)` is a **saddle function**: convex in the resource state `x_t` (Lemma 1) and concave in the price state `y_t` (Lemma 2). Saddle cuts (`y_t^{j⊤} μ_t + φ_t ≥ α_t^j + β_t^{j⊤} x_t`, with `‖μ_t‖_∞ ≤ α`) give a valid lower-bound function. | Ω_t finite and discrete; stagewise-independent *noise*; each stage problem has an optimal solution at all reachable states; `y_t` **exogenous and uncontrolled**, linear recursion, additive noise; a finite `α` exists bounding the saddle-cut dual (their Theorem 1). | Downward, Dowson & Baucke (2020), Lemmas 1–2, Theorem 1. They explicitly *relax* Philpott–Guan's assumption (A1) "so that the random quantities can appear in any part of the optimization problem". |
| 1.8 | The saddle-SDDP convergence result (their Lemma 5) is proved for **Algorithm 1 = full scenario enumeration** (`for s ∈ S`), and relies on the reachable set of `y_t` being **finite** — which it is only because the noise is discrete, with cardinality growing as `∏_{τ≤t} N_τ`. | As 1.7 + enumeration of all scenarios. | Downward et al. (2020), Algorithm 1 and Lemma 5: *"since the stochastic process for y_t is discrete, there are a finite number of possible values for y_t for each t"*. |
| 1.9 | **Markov-chain SDDP / Markovian policy graph.** Dependence is carried by a finite-state Markov chain on *nodes*; each (stage, Markov state) node gets its **own** value function and its own cut pool. Within a node the noise must still be stagewise (nodewise) independent. No extra state variable is needed, and the noise distribution may differ arbitrarily across Markov states. | Dowson (2020) (A1) V finite; (A2) hazard-decision nodes; (A3) Ω_i finite at each node; (A4) **subproblem is an LP** given fixed ω_i, x_i (relaxable to convex under a constraint qualification, per Girardeau et al. 2015); (A5) bounded feasible optimal control at every reachable state (= relatively complete recourse); (A6) positive probability of reaching a leaf (discounting < 1 around any cycle); (A7) outgoing state in a polytope. | Dowson (2020), §Assumptions (A1)–(A7) and §3.1. |
| 1.10 | **Cost of Markov-chain SDDP: cuts are NOT shared across Markov states.** Work per stage multiplies by the number of Markov states; a multi-dimensional exogenous process requires a joint quantisation, so the Markov-state count grows combinatorially in the number of series. | As 1.9. | Downward et al. (2020) §1: discretisation *"is computationally expensive since cuts must be added for all of the discretized Markov states. Moreover, if the process is multi-dimensional, the number of Markov states re[quired]…"* |
| 1.11 | **There is no published convergence proof for the classical Markovian-SDDP algorithms.** | — | Dowson (2020), literature review: *"Various authors (Gjelsvik et al. (1999); Philpott and de Matos (2012)) presented algorithms for the Markovian policy graph setting, although none provided convergence results."* Dowson (2020) himself supplies one, but only under (A1)–(A7), i.e. **LP/convex subproblems**. |
| 1.12 | Cut sharing under interstage dependency is possible for a **restricted class** of dependency models, via cut *adjustment* rather than plain sharing. | Specific interstage-dependency models (principally RHS dependence). | Infanger & Morton (1996), Math. Prog. 75:241–256. |

### 2. Convergence theorems for SDDP and what the reported "gap" certifies

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Almost-sure finite convergence.** A modified SDDP (DOASA) converges with probability 1 in a finite number of iterations, the proof resting on the **finiteness of the set of distinct cut coefficients**. | (i) Ω_t discrete and finite for every t; (ii) noise realisations stagewise independent; (iii) each stage problem has at least one optimal solution for all reachable states (relatively complete recourse + boundedness); (iv) their (A1) restricting where randomness appears (RHS); sampling with replacement. | Philpott & Guan (2008), ORL 36:450–455. Assumptions (i)–(iii) as restated verbatim by Downward, Dowson & Baucke (2020) §2, who note they *relax* (A1). |
| 2.2 | Convergence is to the optimum of the **sampled (SAA) problem**, not of the true problem. SDDP is applied to a finitely generated SAA problem; statistical properties of the SAA and computational properties of SDDP are separate questions. | As 2.1. | Shapiro (2011), §6: *"We discussed here the SDDP method applied to the SAA rather than the original (true) problem. This has an advantage of separating the study of statistical properties of sample average approximations and computational properties of the SDDP algorithm."* |
| 2.3 | Extensions of a.s. convergence: **convex** (not just linear) subproblems on a linear policy graph — Girardeau, Leclère & Philpott (2015); **coherent risk measures** — Guigues (2016); **general acyclic policy graph** — Rebennack (2016); **Markovian/general policy graph with (A1)–(A7)** — Dowson (2020). | Per each paper; all require convexity of the stage problem in the state. | Dowson (2020), literature review. |
| 2.4 | **The lower bound is deterministic and valid.** The first-stage optimal value `θ_k` of the cut-approximated problem under-approximates the SAA optimum, because the cuts are under-approximations of the true expected cost-to-go. | Validity of cuts (⟸ 2.1 assumptions). | Shapiro (2011), §3. |
| 2.5 | **The upper bound is statistical, and is a bound on the value of the *current policy*.** The forward-pass policy `x̄_t(ξ_[t])` is feasible by construction; hence `E[Σ_t c_t^⊤ x̄_t(ξ_[t])]` upper-bounds the optimum. `θ̄ + z_α σ̂_θ/√M` bounds it with confidence ≈ 1−α. Under **subsampling** from the SAA tree it bounds the **SAA** optimum; under **direct sampling from the true law** it bounds the **true** optimum. | M ≳ 30; correct sampling scheme. | Shapiro (2011), §3, eq. (3.15) and following; Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019) §3.1 make the same point for SDDiP. |
| 2.6 | **The reported gap certifies less than it looks.** `ub − θ_k` is an approximate upper bound on the gap between *the value of the current policy* and *the optimal value of the SAA problem*. It does not certify closeness to the true problem's optimum. | As 2.4/2.5. | Shapiro (2011), Remark 4. |
| 2.7 | **The classic stopping rule is provably optimistic.** Stopping when `θ_k` exceeds the lower end of the confidence interval "could be quite misleading": the larger the variance `σ̂_θ²` and the confidence `1−α`, the *sooner* it stops. `σ̂_θ²` grows roughly proportionally with the number of stages T. | — | Shapiro (2011), Remark 4 (criticising the stopping criterion of Pereira & Pinto 1991). Zou et al. (2019) §3.1 concur: *"no matter how large M is, it could still happen that this upper bound is smaller than the valid lower bound."* |
| 2.8 | **The SAA bound is doubly biased.** `E[SAA optimal value] ≤ true optimal value` (minimisation), and `θ_k ≤ SAA optimum`; therefore `E[θ_k]` is a lower bound for the **true** optimum. For a **maximisation** problem the sign flips: the sampled optimum is **optimistically biased**. | i.i.d. sample. | Shapiro (2011), Remark 3. |
| 2.9 | **Taking the best of k upper bounds costs confidence.** `min{ub_1,…,ub_k}` bounds the policy value with probability ≥ 1 − kα (Bonferroni); individual bounds must be computed at level 1 − α/k. | Independent runs. | Shapiro (2011), Remark 5. |
| 2.10 | **Lower-bound stabilisation ≠ optimality.** *"stabilization of the lower bounds θ_k does not mean that the SAA problem is solved to optimality. It simply means that further runs of the SDDP algorithm do not significantly improve the constructed policy."* | — | Shapiro (2011), §6. |
| 2.11 | **Worst-case iteration count is exponential in state dimension.** For two stages, SDDP *is* Kelley's cutting-plane algorithm; there exists a problem where an ε-optimal solution needs at least `(2 ln 2)^{-1} · 1.15^{n-1} · ln(ε^{-1})` oracle calls, n = state dimension. The multistage case can only be worse. | Kelley's method; worst-case instance (Nemirovski/Nesterov construction). | Shapiro (2011), §6, citing Nesterov (2004), pp. 158–160. |
| 2.12 | **Positive complexity result exists but only under stagewise independence.** Complexity of some *deterministic* SDDP variants increases only mildly with T — linearly in T for discounted problems. | "the standard stage-wise independence assumption"; convexity. | Lan, *Complexity of stochastic dual dynamic programming*, Math. Prog. (2022); arXiv:1912.07702. |

### 3. SDDiP (Zou, Ahmed & Sun 2019) — the MILP case

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Why binary states are the pivot.** Any real-valued function on `{0,1}^n` has a convex lower envelope that is convex piecewise-linear on `[0,1]^n` and **coincides with f at every binary point**. Hence a nonconvex expected-cost-to-go function of *binary* states admits an exact convex polyhedral representation. For non-binary states it is in general **impossible** to build a convex polyhedral representation tight at the evaluated state values. | State variables binary. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), Theorem 1 and §2. |
| 3.2 | **Standing assumptions.** (A1) `f_n(x_n,y_n)` linear, `X_n` nonempty **compact** mixed-integer polyhedral set; (A2) **complete continuous recourse** — for any value of the state and local integer variables there exist continuous local variables making the node feasible (achievable by adding penalised nonnegative slacks); (A3) determinism of the MIP solver: same parent solution + same `ψ_n^i` ⇒ same returned optimal `x_n^i` (satisfied by CPLEX/Gurobi; uniqueness of the optimum is *not* assumed). State `x_n ∈ {0,1}^d`; **local** variables `y_n` may be general mixed-integer. | — | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), (A1), (A2), Definition 1, (A3). |
| 3.3 | **Sufficient cut conditions (Definition 2).** A cut family is: **valid** if `Q_n(x_{a(n)}) ≥ v_n^i + (π_n^i)^⊤ x_{a(n)} ∀ x_{a(n)} ∈ {0,1}^d` — note validity is demanded **only on binary points**; **tight** if `Q_n^i(x_{a(n)}^i, ψ_n^{i+1}) = v_n^i + (π_n^i)^⊤ x_{a(n)}^i`, i.e. tight for the **approximate** value function at the forward solution, *not* for the true `Q_n`; **finite** if each backward step generates finitely many distinct `(v_n^i, π_n^i)`. Tightness "alludes to a strong duality property". | — | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), Definition 2, eq. (3.4)–(3.6). |
| 3.4 | **Finite convergence w.p.1 (Theorem 2).** If forward-step sampling is **with replacement**, the backward-step cuts are **valid, tight and finite**, and the nodal solver satisfies (A3), then w.p.1 the forward step defines an **optimal** solution to the MSIP after a **finite** number of iterations. Proof mechanism: `2^d` possible binary states × finitely many cut coefficients ⇒ finitely many realisable polyhedral models; sampling with replacement + finitely many scenarios each with positive probability ⇒ Borel–Cantelli. | (A1)–(A3) + valid/tight/finite cuts + sampling with replacement + finitely many scenarios. Stagewise independence is needed for the *recombining-tree* SDDiP variant (§3.2), not for the SND proof. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), Theorem 2 and proof (Claims 1–2). |
| 3.5 | **Cut-family classification.** | | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), §4.1–4.4. |

| Cut family | valid | tight | finite | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benders (LP-relaxation duals) | ✔ | ✘ (in general) | ✔ (with basic dual optimal solutions) | **No optimality guarantee for MSIP** |
| Integer optimality cuts (Laporte–Louveaux-type, extended) | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | Exact, but "tight at the proposed binary solution x_n^i but could be very loose at other solutions, and thus lead to slow convergence" |
| **Lagrangian cuts** (dual of `z_n = x_{a(n)}` on the local-copy reformulation with `z_n ∈ [0,1]^d`) | ✔ | ✔ (Theorem 3, via strong duality) | ✔ | **Exact** — the guarantee-bearing family |
| Strengthened Benders | ✔ | ✘ (not in general) | ✔ | No guarantee alone; strictly dominates plain Benders on their example |

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.6 | **Tightness of the Lagrangian cut needs exactly two ingredients:** (1) the state `x_{a(n)}` must be **binary**; (2) the local-copy reformulation `z_n = x_{a(n)}`, `z_n ∈ [0,1]^d`, must be the object relaxed (a *direct* Lagrangian relaxation of the original coupling constraints does not give a tight cut). | — | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), §4.3 and Examples 1–2. |
| 3.7 | **Non-binary state ⇒ provable duality gap.** Counterexample: `Q(x) = min{y : 1.5y ≥ x, y ∈ {0,1,2}}`, `x ∈ {0,1,2}`. At `x = 1`, `Q(1) = 1` but the Lagrangian dual value is `2/3`. Tightness fails; Theorem 2 does not apply. | — | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), Example 1, Fig. 1. |
| 3.8 | **Binarisation of general mixed-integer states (Theorem 4).** Under (A1)+(A2) one can build an approximating MSIP with binary states such that **any** optimal solution of the approximation is **ε-optimal** for the original, using per node `k ≤ d(⌊log₂(M√d / ε)⌋ + 1)` binary state variables, `M` a data-dependent constant. Growth is **linear in d, logarithmic in 1/ε**. For general-integer states set `ε = 1`. | (A1) bounded feasible region; (A2) complete continuous recourse. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), Theorem 4. |
| 3.9 | The concern that binarising general integers hurts MIP solvers (Owen & Mehrotra) is argued not to apply: with T stages, N nodes/stage, d state and n local variables per stage, the extensive form has `(1+N^{T-1})n + (T−1)d` variables of which at most `(T−1)d` are binarised — a vanishing fraction. | Extensive-form view. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), §5. (This is an argument, not a theorem — see §2 below.) |

### 4. Deterministic-equivalent MSP: what exact solution certifies

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **Scenario-tree size.** With `N_t` conditional realisations per stage, the SAA problem has `N = ∏_{t=2}^{T} N_t` scenarios. "Even with a moderate number of scenarios per stage, say each `N_t` equals several hundreds, the total number of scenarios grows exponentially and quickly becomes astronomically large." | Conditional sampling; stagewise independence preserves a *recombining* tree. | Shapiro (2011), §3; Shapiro (2006). |
| 4.2 | **What exact solution guarantees:** optimality **with respect to the distribution encoded in the tree**, and nothing more. The tree is a sampled approximation; the statistical link to the true law is a separate theorem (4.3–4.5). | — | Shapiro (2011), §6. |
| 4.3 | **Two-stage SAA sample size.** Under mild regularity, `N ≥ (O(1)σ²/ε²)·[ n·log(DL/ε) + log(O(1)/δ) ]` guarantees that any ε/2-optimal solution of the SAA problem is ε-optimal for the true problem w.p. ≥ 1−δ. Grows **linearly in the first-stage dimension n**, proportional to `σ²/ε²`. Requires `F(x,ξ)` real-valued for all `x, ξ` — which "can only hold if the corresponding recourse is relatively complete". | Finite diameter D; Lipschitz f with constant L; light tails (`log M_x(t) ≤ σ²t²/2`); relatively complete recourse. Shapiro's Example 1 shows the estimate cannot be significantly improved. | Shapiro (2006), eq. (2)–(3), citing Kleywegt–Shapiro–Homem-de-Mello (2001). |
| 4.4 | **Multistage SAA sample size (Theorem 2).** Conditional sampling with `N_1 = N_2` and `O(1)(DL/ε)^{n_1+n_2} exp(−O(1)N_1ε²/σ²) ≤ δ` gives ε-optimality w.p. ≥ 1−δ. Since total scenarios = `N_1·N_2`, the **number of scenarios needed grows exponentially with T**. | Three-stage setting extended straightforwardly; **between-stages independence assumed**; Lipschitz constants independent of the noise; light tails. | Shapiro (2006), Theorem 2 and eq. (28). |
| 4.5 | **Honest caveat in the primary source.** *"the above analysis does not prove in a rigorous mathematical sense that complexity of multistage programming grows exponentially with increase of the number of stages."* And explicitly: *"Removing the between-stages independence assumption may create technical difficulties and requires a further investigation."* | — | Shapiro (2006), §4. |
| 4.6 | **Rate of convergence.** Optimal values of SAA problems converge to their true counterparts at rate `O_p(N^{-1/2})`. | i.i.d. sample from the true distribution. | Shapiro (2011), §2 (Remark following Remark 3). |
| 4.7 | **Consequence for a *reduced, re-weighted* ensemble.** All of 4.3–4.6 presuppose an i.i.d. sample drawn from the true law. A scenario set produced by reduction/clustering with explicit non-uniform weights is **not** such a sample, so none of these bounds apply to it. What remains provable for it is 4.2 only: exact optimality for the written-down discrete distribution. (Stability of the optimal value under perturbation of the measure is a different theory — probability-metric/Fortet–Mourier stability, Dupačová–Gröwe-Kuska–Römisch — and it requires the reduction to be near-optimal *in that metric*, a constructed property, not a free one.) | — | Logical corollary of 4.3–4.6 (Shapiro 2006/2011). The stability-theory pointer is **not re-verified in this pass** — treat the specific reference as a lead. |

### 5. ADP / value-function approximation for storage

| # | Claim | Exact assumptions | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **SPAR (Separable Projective Approximation Routine).** Learning algorithm for separable concave piecewise-linear approximations; converges when all intervals (segments) are sampled infinitely often; produces the optimal solution when only the optimal solution is sampled. Concavity is maintained by a **projection step** (monotonicity of slopes). | Separability; concavity; infinitely-often sampling of every segment. | Powell, Ruszczyński & Topaloglu (2004), MOR 29(4):814–836. |
| 5.2 | **Unbiased-gradient result.** If at each iteration an unbiased estimate of a segment's gradient is obtained, then provided the probability of visiting each segment has a strictly positive lower bound in the limit, the estimate converges to the true value function almost surely. | Unbiased gradient estimates; positive visit probability floor. | Powell et al. (2004) / SPAR family. |
| 5.3 | **First multistage convergence proof with piecewise-linear VFA.** Convergence of a **purely exploitative** ADP algorithm given a **concave piecewise-linear** value function, for the lagged asset acquisition problem. The exploitation-only property is what concavity buys: no exploration policy is needed. Restricted to a **totally ordered (scalar) resource state**. | Concave PWL value function; scalar resource; problem-specific structure of the lagged-acquisition problem. | Nascimento & Powell (2009), MOR 34(1):210–237. Characterised by Jiang & Powell: *"the authors prove convergence of a purely exploitative ADP algorithm given a concave, piecewise–linear value function… in our algorithm we assume exploration of all states, a requirement that can be avoided when we are able to assume concavity"*; and *"we consider the case where S may not be a total ordering… which was not allowed in Nascimento and Powell [2009]."* |
| 5.4 | **Concave scalar storage with vector-valued controls.** Convergence proof for an approximate-value-iteration (TD(0)) ADP algorithm for high-dimensional stochastic control problems linked by a **scalar** storage device, using a piecewise-linear (concave) VFA with adaptive discretisation, **given a technical condition**. Explicitly exploits "the natural concavity of the problem to avoid any need for explicit exploration policies". | Concavity in the scalar resource; **scalar** storage state; vector controls; a stated technical condition (not re-extracted in this pass — the paper is paywalled); stepsize of the form `α_0/(α_0+n)`. | Nascimento & Powell (2013), IEEE TAC 58(12):2995–3010, doi:10.1109/TAC.2013.2272973. |
| 5.5 | **Monotone-ADP convergence theorem — the fully explicit assumption list** (useful as the template for what any such proof needs). Under Assumptions 5.2–5.6, `V̄_t^n(s) → V_t^*(s)` almost surely for every t, s. Proof: Tsitsiklis (1994) shrinking-rectangle argument. | **5.1** monotonicity: terminal `C_T` monotone, and `(HV)_t` monotone whenever `V_{t+1}` is. **5.2** exploration: `Σ_n P(S_t^n = s ∣ F^{n-1}) = ∞` a.s. for every state s and t < T ⇒ every state visited infinitely often (extended Borel–Cantelli). **5.3** bounded contributions `0 ≤ C_t(s,a) ≤ C_max`. **5.4** bounded observations `0 ≤ v̂_t^n(s) ≤ V_max` a.s. **5.5** **martingale-difference noise**: `E[w_t^{n+1}(s) ∣ F^n] = 0`. **5.6** stepsizes `α_t^n ∈ [0,1]`, `F^n`-measurable, `Σ α_t^n = ∞` a.s., `Σ (α_t^n)² < ∞` a.s. | Jiang & Powell, *An ADP Algorithm for Monotone Value Functions*, arXiv:1401.1590 (published Operations Research, 2015). |
| 5.6 | **Concavity substitutes for exploration; monotonicity does not.** Monotone-ADP needs Assumption 5.2 (visit every state infinitely often); the concavity-preserving algorithms (SPAR, Nascimento–Powell 2009 lagged acquisition, Topaloglu–Powell leveling) do not, because a concavity-preserving projection is "the same as maintaining monotonicity in the slopes" and propagates information from a visited segment to all others. | Concavity. | Jiang & Powell, §1 and §6 preamble. |
| 5.7 | **Bias kills 5.5.** When the approximation scheme is a sample mean and the supremum sits outside the expectation, bias can *"cause Assumption 5.5 to be unsatisfied"*. I.e. any unmodelled dependence that makes `v̂` conditionally biased voids the a.s. convergence theorem. | — | Jiang & Powell, §5 discussion of Assumption 5.5. |
| 5.8 | **Integer local variables destroy convexity/concavity of the value function.** *"in the presence of integer local variables, the value functions and expected cost-to-go functions are nonconvex with respect to the state variables."* Enforcing concavity by projection therefore yields an approximation with **no proven bound** relative to the true value function. | Integer local variables present. | Zou–Ahmed–Sun (2019), §2. |

### 6. Head-to-head: what is provably correct under strongly cross-correlated, stagewise-DEPENDENT exogenous processes

| Method | Requirement on the uncertainty representation | Provable guarantee under stagewise dependence + cross-correlation | Degradation when the assumption fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deterministic-equivalent MSP** (scenario tree / fan, solved as one MILP) | **None on the dependence structure.** Dependence and cross-correlation are just properties of the joint law that the tree encodes. Needs only: finite discrete support, explicit weights. | **Exact optimality for the distribution written down** (4.2). This is the *only* one of the three whose correctness is untouched by stagewise dependence. | Nothing breaks in correctness. Only size (4.1) and the statistical link to the true law (4.3–4.7). |
| **SDDP** (LP cuts) | Stagewise independence, or dependence pushed into an augmented state (RHS-only, linear AR), or into Markov states. Convexity of the stage problem in the state. | **Nothing** under raw stagewise dependence: cuts are invalid (1.3), so the lower bound bounds nothing and Philpott–Guan/Girardeau/Guigues do not apply. With RHS-AR augmentation: full 2.1 guarantee restored, at `+p·dim(b)` state dimensions and worst-case exponential-in-dimension iteration count (2.11). With objective dependence: **no valid LP cuts exist** (1.5). | Becomes a heuristic that emits a number labelled "lower bound" which is not a bound. |
| **Saddle SDDP** (Downward et al.) | Price state exogenous, **linear** recursion with additive noise, entering the objective bilinearly; convex-in-resource / concave-in-price. | a.s. finite convergence — but the proof is for **full scenario enumeration** (1.8), i.e. the tractability advantage of SDDP is surrendered in the theorem. | Non-linear or endogenous price dynamics ⇒ saddle structure lost ⇒ no valid bounding function. |
| **Markov-chain SDDP** | Exogenous process representable as a finite-state Markov chain; **LP/convex** subproblems (A4). | Under Dowson (2020) (A1)–(A7): a.s. convergence on a Markovian policy graph. For the classical Gjelsvik/Philpott–de Matos algorithms: **no convergence proof exists** (1.11). | The Markov chain is a *quantisation*; the policy is optimal for the quantised process, and no bound is proven relating it to the unquantised one. Joint quantisation of K correlated series multiplies the value-function count (1.10). |
| **SDDiP** | Binary state variables; (A1) compact MI polyhedral, (A2) complete continuous recourse, (A3) deterministic solver; valid+tight+finite cuts; sampling with replacement. Stagewise independence only for the recombining-tree variant. | The **only cut method that can absorb objective-side and LHS stagewise dependence** — because after binary expansion the induced bilinear terms are products of binaries and linearise exactly (1.6). Finite convergence w.p.1 to the MSIP optimum (3.4). | With non-binary states, Lagrangian cuts lose tightness and a provable duality gap opens (3.7) ⇒ Theorem 2 void. With only Benders/strengthened-Benders cuts: no optimality guarantee (3.5). |
| **ADP with concave PWL VFA** | Concavity in a **scalar** resource; exogenous information either i.i.d. or fully captured in the state; unbiased (martingale-difference) observations. | **Nothing** when the exogenous process is stagewise dependent and not part of the state: `V(resource)` is not a sufficient statistic, `v̂` becomes conditionally biased, Assumption 5.5 fails (5.7), and 5.3–5.5 do not apply. | Degrades to a projection onto the concave PWL class: a *policy*, with no bound and no certificate. It is the value function of a **misspecified** MDP. |

**Blunt ranking for the stated condition (strong cross-correlation, stagewise dependence, MILP stages):** deterministic-equivalent MSP > SDDiP with Lagrangian cuts (only after binarising both the state *and* the dependence structure) > Markov-chain SDDP (no proof for MILP subproblems) > ADP concave VFA (no proof at all once the exogenous state is dropped) > plain SDDP (cuts invalid).

## Applicability to the target system

Target: BTM battery; gates S0 (slow loop producing `V`) → S1 aFRR capacity → S2 day-ahead → S3 post-DA rebalance → S4 continuous intraday; ONE MILP family per gate with a commitment ledger fixing variables; terminal `V(SOC, peakState, qualState)` concave PWL in SOC; uncertainty = ONE joint ensemble `[S=64 × H=192 × K series]` with non-uniform weights and a shared scenario axis; binaries for import/export complementarity, a MIN linearisation, and a reserve-bid grid.

### Assumptions the target system VIOLATES, and the cost

| Assumption | Violated by | Cost / what is lost |
|---|---|---|
| **Stagewise independence** (1.1, 2.1, 3.4-tree variant, 4.4) | Deliberately. Cross-series correlation across prices/load/PV/aFRR "is the whole point"; electricity prices and load are strongly autocorrelated over 15-min slots. | Every SDDP-family guarantee evaporates unless dependence is moved into state or Markov nodes. **Cut sharing across the 192 slots is invalid** (1.3). |
| **Uncertainty in the RHS only** (1.4, Philpott–Guan A1) | Prices multiply energy decisions ⇒ uncertainty is in the **objective coefficients**. | The cheap AR state-augmentation route (1.4) is **closed** for LP-SDDP: bilinear terms, non-convex stage problem (1.5). Remaining options: saddle SDDP (1.7, whose proof needs enumeration — 1.8) or SDDiP's binary-expansion linearisation (1.6). |
| **Convexity/concavity of the value function in the state** (1.9 A4, 2.1, 2.3, 5.1–5.6) | Per-slot import/export complementarity binaries, a MIN linearisation, a reserve-bid grid ⇒ MILP stage problems. | `Q_n` is nonconvex in the state (5.8). **Plain SDDP cuts are invalid, not merely loose.** Dowson (2020) (A4) requires an LP subproblem ⇒ the only Markovian-SDDP convergence proof does not cover this model. |
| **Binary state variables** (3.1, 3.3, 3.6, 3.7) | SOC is continuous; peakState/qualState are discrete but not necessarily binary. | Must binarise via Theorem 4 (3.8). Cheap in *count* — with `d` ≈ 3 and ε at, say, 1% of capacity, `k ≤ d(⌊log₂(M√d/ε)⌋+1)` is roughly 10–25 binaries per node — but each backward step then needs a Lagrangian dual solved by repeated MILP solves. **Without binarisation, Lagrangian cuts are provably not tight (3.7) and Theorem 2 is void.** |
| **Complete continuous recourse** (A2, 3.2) | Not automatic: an infeasible SOC/peak/qual combination is reachable. | Achievable by construction — add penalised nonnegative slacks (3.2). **Cheap, and mandatory** for both 3.4 and 4.3. Should be an explicit spec requirement, not an accident of the model. |
| **i.i.d. sample from the true law** (4.3, 4.4, 4.6, 2.5, 2.8) | `S=64` **reduced** scenarios with **explicit non-uniform weights**. | Neither Shapiro (2006) eq. (2) / Theorem 2 nor the `O_p(N^{-1/2})` rate (4.6) applies. The statistical upper bound of 2.5 is **not** a bound on the true optimum — only on the optimum of the reduced distribution. **The only surviving guarantee is 4.2.** |
| **Sampling with replacement over finitely many scenarios** (3.4) | The ensemble is fixed and enumerable (64 scenarios). | Actually *helpful*: with 64 scenarios the fan can be enumerated, so the Borel–Cantelli step in Theorem 2 is trivially satisfied — or bypassed by full enumeration (deterministic equivalent). |
| **Scalar resource state, concavity, exogenous info in-state, martingale-difference observations** (5.3–5.7) | `V` is conditioned on `(peakState, qualState)` — so the state is *not* scalar, it is scalar-per-discrete-cell. Prices/load/PV/aFRR are stagewise dependent and are **not** in the state of `V`. Binaries make the true `V(SOC)` nonconcave (5.8). | Nascimento–Powell (2009/2013) proofs apply **per discrete cell** only if within a cell the state is genuinely scalar and the exogenous process is i.i.d. Neither holds. Assumption 5.5 fails (5.7). The concavity-preserving projection produces a `V` with **no proven relation to the true value function**. |
| **Genuine multistage non-anticipativity** | The `[S × H]` ensemble with a **shared scenario axis** is a **fan**, not a recombining tree: each of the 64 scenarios is a full 192-slot path. Enforcing non-anticipativity per gate makes each gate problem a **two-stage** (here-and-now / wait-and-see) SAA problem, not an MSP. | This is the honest characterisation of the target: **a sequence of two-stage SAA problems glued by a terminal value function**, not a multistage stochastic program. That is *good news* — the two-stage SAA theory (4.3) is far stronger than the multistage theory (4.4) — but it means the target does **not** produce a multistage policy; it produces a first-stage decision per gate. Any claim of multistage optimality would be false. |

### Consequences, ranked

1. **The right frame for the target is 4.2 + 4.3, not SDDP.** One MILP over `[64 × 192 × K]` with a concave PWL terminal `V` added to a maximisation objective is a deterministic-equivalent (fan) formulation. Its guarantee: **exactly optimal here-and-now decision for the 64-point weighted distribution, given `V`**. That is a real, checkable guarantee and it requires nothing of the dependence structure. It is strictly stronger than anything SDDP can give on this model.
2. **`V` is exogenous to the planner, so the method question is really "what produces `V`".** The three candidate producers:
   - **SDDiP with Lagrangian cuts** (binarised SOC/peak/qual, complete continuous recourse via penalised slacks). This is the only route with a **finite convergence w.p.1 to the true MILP optimum** (3.4) that survives both the binaries and (via 1.6) objective-side dependence. Price: binarise the state and the AR structure; solve a Lagrangian dual per node per iteration.
   - **Markov-chain SDDP** on a joint quantisation of the K series. Buys arbitrary cross-correlation and non-Gaussian marginals per Markov state (1.9), costs one value function per (slot, Markov state) with no cut sharing (1.10), and has **no convergence proof for MILP subproblems** — Dowson (2020) (A4) is an LP.
   - **ADP concave PWL VFA** (SPAR/CAVE/leveling, Nascimento–Powell). Matches the *shape* the target wants exactly — concave PWL in scalar SOC, one curve per discrete cell — and needs no exploration if concavity truly holds (5.6). But its convergence theorems do **not** apply here (5.7, 5.8). Use it as a *heuristic estimator of a concave envelope*, and say so.
3. **The concavity of the true `V(SOC)` is a checkable property, not a licence.** If the import/export complementarity binaries are non-binding at the optimum (objective already discourages simultaneous import/export) and the MIN linearisation and reserve-bid grid are exact at the LP relaxation, then the stage problem's LP relaxation is exact and concavity of `V` in SOC is recovered — restoring the entire SDDP/ADP toolbox. **Test this before choosing a method.** If the binaries bind, `V` is genuinely nonconcave (5.8) and the concave PWL `V` is an approximation whose error is unbounded by any theorem cited here.
4. **Report the gap honestly.** If any SDDP/SDDiP loop is used to produce `V`: the lower bound is deterministic and valid for the *sampled, reduced* problem only; the upper bound is statistical and bounds the *current policy* (2.5, 2.6); the classic "stop when the LB enters the CI" rule is provably optimistic (2.7); LB stabilisation is not optimality (2.10); and for a **maximisation** objective the sampled optimum is **optimistically** biased (2.8). None of the published bounds certifies distance to the true (unreduced, unquantised) optimum.
5. **192 stages is the binding constraint on any MSP guarantee.** A genuine 192-stage recombining tree with even 2 branches per stage is `2^191` scenarios (4.1). The target's 64-scenario fan is not that tree; it is a 64-point discretisation of the joint path law. Do not describe it as a multistage tree.
